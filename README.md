## The challenge

By my estimate, roughly three quarters of a project manager's week goes to administrative load. The work that decides whether a project succeeds, managing risk and the relationships around it, competes with that load for the same hours.

The largest contractors and their software platforms have already moved. The owner's side is only beginning to. On most project teams, a tool still arrives as a license and a login, with little direction on what it is for. Project managers are not resistant. Most have not been told which work AI does, which work it helps with, and which work stays with them.

## The approach

AI belongs first in the work that prepares a decision. It drafts the status report, reconciles the cost report, and reviews a change order or pay application against the contract before the project manager picks it up.

That is the near-term value. The larger one comes later, when the same reviews run on their own and share what they find. A layer that reads across every party's records can connect a pending decision to the milestone it threatens while there is still time to act.

The path is deliberate. Automate the repeatable work, build targeted reviews next, and connect them over time. The project manager makes every decision along the way.

## What changed along the way

The early drafts leaned on the reader to connect the dots. The opening summary did not carry the full thesis, and the business case was left implied by everything around it. I rewrote the introduction so it stands on its own and gave the business case its own page.

I also learned to show more and describe less. The prototypes were screenshots until I recorded the walkthrough. The synthesis layer was a paragraph until I drew one dependency running across three companies' records, and that drawing did more than the paragraph ever did. A few graphics that did not match the text around them were redrawn or cut.

## The prototypes

The report describes three review tools I built on my own time, a change order reviewer, a pay application reviewer, and a schedule risk analyzer. Each uses the same split. AI interprets the document, code checks the math and the rules, and the project manager decides.

The video above shows all three running on test documents. The test documents and the reviews each tool produced are in the [prototypes repo](https://github.com/prentice-builds/construction-review-prototypes).

## What this is and is not

It is one practitioner's point of view, drawn from fifteen years as an owner's representative and three years teaching myself to build with AI.

The time figures are estimates from my own experience, not measured data. The report says so, and it argues that a baseline is the first step for any team.

The prototypes demonstrate an approach and an architecture. They were tested on documents I constructed and they are not a production system.
